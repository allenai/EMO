Interpretation

What the sweep supports

For this model, dataset, token budget, and 20-step warmup, WSD consistently improves training CE, held-out C4 CE, and BPB at matched LR.

Held-out C4 CE selects cosine 2.5e-4 and WSD 5e-4, though WSD’s 0.000803 advantage over 2.5e-4 weakly identifies its precise optimum.

What remains uncertain

HellaSwag differences are small, seed variance is unmeasured, and the LR optimum differs by objective.

This result should be treated as a one-epoch finding, not yet a claim about longer training or smaller models.

Method and metric definitions

Training: all 12 runs used a dense 1B model, the 1B-token codelion/dclm-baseline-1B mixture, 239 optimizer steps, eight H100s, 20 warmup steps, and commit f6073a59.

Final training CE: unsmoothed train/CE loss from the exact W&B history row at _step=239.

Held-out validation CE: eval/heldout/c4_en-validation/CE loss uses the fixed 65-batch C4 validation array with 109,648 label tokens.

HellaSwag: eval/downstream/hellaswag (length-normalized accuracy) is evaluated after training, while the logged v2 value is identical for every run.

HellaSwag BPB: eval/downstream/hellaswag (BPB) is the primary non-v2 confidence metric normalized by bytes, and lower is better.

Raw HellaSwag accuracy: an unnormalized accuracy metric was not emitted by this evaluator, so the report uses the requested primary length-normalized accuracy only.

Verification: terminal state, step count, and rounded metrics were cross-checked against Beaker execution logs; exact values come from W&B summaries/history.
